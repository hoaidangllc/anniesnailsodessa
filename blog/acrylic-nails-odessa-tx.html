Acrylic nail guide

Acrylic nails, without the guesswork.

What a full set is, when you may need a fill, how to choose a shape, and what to know before you book.

What is an acrylic full set?

An acrylic enhancement is created by combining liquid and powder to build structure over the nail. A full set can add length, reshape the silhouette of the nail and create a strong base for color or design. The final result can be short and natural-looking or longer and more dramatic.

If you work with your hands, type all day, care for children, or simply prefer low-maintenance nails, tell your technician. A shorter almond, round or square set can be much easier to live with than choosing a length only because it looks good in a photo.

Full set or fill?

A full set may make sense when

You are starting fresh, changing the structure substantially, or the existing product has lifting, damage or growth that makes rebuilding the set the better option.

A fill may make sense when

The existing enhancement is still in good condition and the main issue is new growth near the cuticle. Your technician can look at the set and tell you what is appropriate.

Don't force a fill on a damaged set. If there is significant lifting, cracking or trapped moisture, covering the problem is not the goal. Let the technician inspect it first.

Choosing a shape that fits your life

Square gives a crisp, classic edge and works especially well at shorter lengths. Almond visually elongates the fingers and has softer corners. Coffin creates a bold tapered profile and usually needs enough length to show the shape. Round is simple, wearable and practical for clients who want less corner exposure.

Bring inspiration photos if you have them, but expect the final shape to be adapted to your nail beds, desired length and daily routine. That is usually a better result than trying to duplicate a photo exactly.

How long do acrylic nails last?

There is no single wear time that applies to everyone. Natural nail growth, length, daily activity, product condition and how hard you are on your hands all matter. Instead of waiting until a set is heavily grown out or lifting, pay attention to how it looks and feels and schedule maintenance when needed.

Do not pick or peel acrylic off at home. That can remove layers of the natural nail along with the product. Annie's menu lists acrylic removal as a separate service, so ask about removal when booking if you already have product on your nails.

Acrylic, dip or gel?

Choose based on what you need rather than which service sounds most popular. Acrylic is useful when structure and added length are the priority. Dip is often chosen for durable color over natural nails. Gel color is a lighter option when you mainly want a polished, glossy manicure without building significant length.

Questions clients often ask

Can I bring a nail photo?

Yes. Inspiration photos are useful for communicating color, shape and design. The technician can explain what can be adapted to your nails.

Does $50+ include every design?

No. It is a starting price for Full Set Shellac. Length, shape, custom art, repairs, removal and other add-ons can change the final price.

What if I already have acrylic on?

Let the salon know, especially if the existing set needs removal or has lifting or damage. That helps allow the right service and time.
