NAIL DESIGN GUIDE

Choose a nail design you will still love after the photo.

Trends move fast. A better starting point is your lifestyle, preferred length, shape and how much detail you want to maintain.

Micro French

A fine tip gives French a lighter, cleaner look and works well on shorter lengths.

Soft ombré

A gradual blend can feel polished without relying on heavy art on every nail.

Chrome accents

Use chrome on all nails for impact or as an accent when you want the effect to stay subtle.

Minimal details

Fine lines, dots and small accents can add personality while keeping the set easy to wear.

Seasonal color

You can make a set feel current simply through color—no complicated art required.

Mixed finishes

Glossy, shimmer and accent textures can create contrast even within one color family.

Shape changes the whole design

The same French tip looks different on square, almond and coffin nails. Choose shape before obsessing over the art. If you work with your hands or type all day, a practical length may matter more than what looks dramatic in a reference photo.

Bring inspiration—but expect adaptation

A reference photo is useful for color, layout and overall mood. Your natural nail width, current length and chosen service may mean the design needs to be adjusted. That is normal.

Ask about design pricing first

Detailed art, length, shape, repairs and specialty finishes can affect the total. Annie's public menu lists starting prices for the base services; custom design pricing depends on what you request.

Best way to prepare: save two or three reference photos, decide which detail matters most, and show them before the service begins. That gives the technician a clearer target than saying “something trendy.”
